## Where the promo field is, and why yours did not apply

The code goes in at checkout, after you have picked a plan and a region. Look for a field labelled **Promotional Code**, paste, hit Apply, and the order total updates in place. If the number does not move, one of these is usually the reason:

1. **The plan is below VM-1.** VM-0.5 and VM-0.75 are excluded by the code's own eligibility rule, and they are already the cheapest things in the catalog.
2. **A code is already in the order.** Evoxt accepts one per order. A user who tried to stack a Black Friday deal with a second code reported it simply did not work.
3. **Case or whitespace.** Paste the string exactly as written. `EVOXT595` is not the same as `evoxt595` or `EVOXT595 `.
4. **The field is missing entirely.** Some promotions are applied to eligible orders automatically rather than through a code, in which case there is nothing to type.

## Who should not buy Evoxt

The service is unmanaged. If you want someone to configure nginx for you, this is the wrong catalog, and the price advantage disappears the moment you pay for managed support elsewhere.

Ticket response runs 4 to 8 hours. Discord and Telegram move faster, but a production incident that needs a human in under four hours is a real risk here. The company has been operating since 2020, which is shorter than the established providers, and that matters if you are putting something genuinely mission-critical on it.

DigitalOcean and Vultr remain the safer pick when you need a large tutorial ecosystem and a longer track record, and users comparing the two generally describe Evoxt as cheaper for equivalent specs, in one case about 20% below a prior High Frequency VPS bill. Smaller Evoxt plans are also available in fewer regions, DNS management is functional but less intuitive than the rest of the panel, and the upgrade path is buried enough that users have had to hunt for it.

Where Evoxt fits well: game servers (Minecraft, Ark and Valheim come up repeatedly in user reports and are described as stable), database-backed web apps that care about single-core speed, development and staging boxes, and anything where a $3.59 monthly bill beats a $12 one.
